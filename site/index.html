6-max no-limit hold'em · openpoker.ai · Rust, CPU only

Five bots that learn from every hand, and change only on evidence.

SvanBot plays on openpoker.ai as five bots under one owner. Every decision is made in-process from the bot's own record of what it has seen. A new way to play reaches the table only after it has beaten the old one on the same cards.

The Arena's pinned run

The champion against the pricer and the sparring archetypes, heads up. Each deal is played twice from mirrored seats so the luck of the cards cancels, and each bar is the 95% interval of what the champion won, in milli-big-blinds per 100 hands.

The pinned run is published with the site. On a local copy, read it in the repo's README.

How a bot decides

  1. 1

    The spot

    Our cards, the board, the pot, the legal actions, our position, and every opponent's line through the hand, by seat.

  2. 2

    The ranges

    The action model gives each move a probability by street, by whether a bet is faced, and by hand strength. A range follows from a line by Bayes' rule, and each opponent's own read tilts it as evidence comes in.

  3. 3

    The decision

    Heads up after the flop, the re-solve solves the rest of the street over both whole ranges by Discounted CFR. Everywhere else, the pricer values each legal action in chips over the deals it samples, including the pot a raise takes when the table folds.

  4. 4

    The action

    The nearest legal action reaches the venue, echoing its hand and turn ids, inside the action clock. The work is an input: samples, iterations and a seed, never a clock.

How it changes

The fleet plays by one parameter set, the champion, named by its digest. A challenger meets it first in a paired sim: the same deals from mirrored seats. Only a challenger whose whole interval is above zero reaches the canary.

The canary, SurSvan, then plays one arm an orbit for 3,000 live hands, with the luck of its own draws taken out. Cleared, the other four adopt the challenger between hands. Harmful, nobody plays it again.

Fair play is absolute

  • Two stablemates never share a table. The seat guard sees every seat list and leaves at the first that seats one.
  • A shared hand is an incident: kept in the store, logged at crit.
  • Each bot's reads are its own, from its own store.
  • Only legal actions reach the venue.

The ship bar

Stage 1

The Arena

Heads up and 6-max, bout by bout, through an anytime-valid 95% confidence sequence. It passes once v10.0.2 is wholly beaten in both, and refuses at once if any row is wholly below zero, or at 48 hours of box CPU.

Stage 2

The canary

SurSvan plays the release first. After 20,000 hands, the canary less the fleet must not be wholly below zero.

Stage 3

The operator

The release is tagged, built by CI, checksummed, and installed canary first. The operator says when.

Six layers, four of them pure

core→ sim→ strategy→ learn→ runtime→ apps

A crate depends only on its own layer or a lower one. The four pure layers touch no network, no file, no store and no clock, so every sim plays again bit for bit. Two checks hold that on every commit.

Why these two answers can differ

Reference

The repo's opening range and pricer rules: a class opens when its rank against the players behind is inside the position's share, and a raise is priced from the hand's own equity against the live opponents.

Blueprint

The opening decision reads a precomputed per-bot bucket table. The boundary bucket opens with a chance, so a 19% range is not rounded to 25%. When facing a raise, the hand still knows its own cards; the frozen per-bot thresholds price the call, and the raise test asks for a 3% price edge where the reference asks for 8%.

The clock

A table read is about 15 ns; a full blueprint decision is about 29 ns. The action clock is 45,000 ms.

Blueprint for one bot

Each bot measures its own view of the shipped preflop table, adds its own sampling noise, and cuts that view into eight equal-mass strength buckets. The table below is that bot's frozen blueprint.

Buckets

Bucket 0 is the strongest view; bucket 7 the weakest. Equity columns are the combo-weighted average of the classes in the bucket, against 1 and 5 random hands.

Opening table

The share of the position's opening range, spread across the boundary bucket. “open” means the whole bucket opens; a percentage is the chance stored for that bucket.

Defence table

What the bucket does facing a normal open (2.5 bb) and a 3-bet (7.5 bb), as the share of the bucket's classes. The class's own equity decides inside the bucket; the table is a view of the frozen thresholds, not a separate per-bucket entry.

Margin study

A paired 6-max ring: every deal is played once from every seat with the blueprint in that seat, and once with the reference in it. The cards are the same, so the deal's luck cancels. A bout is 50 deals. The row difference is summed over the bout, pooled over rows, and read through the repo's confidence sequence. Reference against itself is zero on every deal by construction, which is the pairing's sanity check.

The question, and the answer this prototype can support

The question. A per-bot, precomputed preflop blueprint: does the abstraction fit inside the venue's 45-second action clock, and does the policy stay inside the 20 bb/100 margin of the reference over paired 6-max rows?
What this prototype is. The blueprint is the reference policy frozen through each bot's own eight-bucket abstraction: the opening range is bucket-level with a mixed boundary, the price response uses the hand's own equity. This is the representation test, not an MCCFR solve. It is the cheap end of E4: if the representation and the lookup fail, a solve cannot rescue them; if they pass, a solve is worth costing.
What is missing from a full E4 answer. A real MCCFR solve, all of the Arena's 6-max rows (v10.0.2 and the learning/switching archetypes are not here), postflop continuation values, exploiters, and AIVAT/luck correction. The GO below is for the abstraction and the clock, not for a solved blueprint's strength.

How the margin read is computed

differences[b] = mean_over_rows(blueprint_bout_sum[b] - reference_bout_sum[b]) × (100,000 / 50)
sequence = Sequence(tuned_for = 200)
mean = sequence.mean()
radius = sequence.radius(alpha = 0.05)
if bouts < 30:                  UNRESOLVED
else if mean + radius < -20,000: REFUSED
else if radius > 20,000:         UNRESOLVED
else:                            ADMITTED

This is learn::margin::pooled followed by learn::margin::read. The unit is mbb/100. A bout's row sum is in big blinds won over its 50 deals, so the multiplication by 2,000 is the conversion the repo uses.

What the prototype models, and what it does not

Modelled

  • Preflop only, 6-max, 10/20 blinds, 2,000-chip stacks, no postflop betting.
  • The repo's opening shares, 2.5 bb open, 3× / 2.3× re-raises, and the shipped 169 × 5 equity table.
  • One abstraction per bot: its own jittered view of the table, eight equal-mass buckets.
  • Paired cards: every deal is played from all six seats for both policies.
  • Four independent rows: pricer, station, nit, maniac.
  • The repo's bout size, confidence sequence, minimum bouts and margin rule.

Not modelled

  • A real MCCFR/DCFR solve; this blueprint is a frozen abstraction of the reference.
  • v10.0.2, the switching and learning archetypes, and the whole Arena row set.
  • Postflop play, leaf values, and the production pricer's sampled EV/fold-equity model.
  • Exploiters, action-model fits, and AIVAT/luck correction beyond card pairing.
  • Network latency and the venue's real action-clock queue.
  • All-in side pots; the prototype caps re-raises at four and never reaches a side pot.

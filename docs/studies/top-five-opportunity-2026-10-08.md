## Answer

The real opportunity controls are initial stack size, between-hand cash-out/requeue decisions, rebuy recovery and useful playing time. Public stakes and the destination table are server-controlled. A client can reject an assigned table and queue again; it cannot request a particular rival or a higher stake through the documented public protocol. Changes should target the weakest bot's opportunity and downside, with measured waiting costs, rather than assume high-ranked opponents are profitable. [Message catalog](https://docs.openpoker.ai/api-reference/message-types/#join_lobby), [public game rules](https://docs.openpoker.ai/platform/game-rules/#format).

## Score, resets and current evidence

Public scoring currently adds off-table balance and table chips; the configured rebuy penalty is zero. Qualification is at least ten hands. Use the API's score because the penalty can change by season. Public tie ordering is not stated in the scoring or season pages; private-competition tie rules do not establish public behavior. [Scoring](https://docs.openpoker.ai/compete/scoring/).

Seasons last fourteen days. Five minutes before the end, new hands stop; existing hands finish. Tables cash out, the leaderboard freezes, and the next season starts with fresh 5,000 chips on registration. The client additionally suppresses voluntary moves during the final fifteen minutes. Durable control therefore requires recovery after each reset, rather than carrying this season's chip cushion forward. [Season lifecycle](https://docs.openpoker.ai/compete/how-seasons-work/#season-lifecycle), [starting chips](https://docs.openpoker.ai/compete/how-seasons-work/#starting-chips), [client season clock][clock].

## Feasible alternatives and evidence needed

1. **Measure opportunity before changing selection.** Compare the existing top-rank preference with neutral retention and evidence-based table quality in an offline arrival/queue model. Record per-bot hand rate, rejected-table reasons, requeue time, opponent effective stacks, adjusted chip growth and maximum drawdown. Rank alone does not measure exploitability; top-rank seeking can displace a soft table. The current code explicitly gives seeking precedence over the tough-table escape. [Seat lifecycle][seat], [quality][quality].
2. **Evaluate banking and buy-in jointly.** Compare the existing threshold/ceiling with a capped-exposure alternative across season-start and mature-season conditions, retaining exact reset and queue costs. Use paired full-session simulation, not just independent hand EV; keep the repository's unchanged promotion gate and report leader downside separately. A per-bot intervention would need explicit isolation because current opportunity configuration is shared by each process. [Config][config], [ADR 0002][adr].
3. **Verify useful uptime and funding recovery first.** Aggregate time waiting for opponents, scheduled rebuy, cash-out, reconnect and local work separately. Fault-injection tests should cover subminimum cash-out without `busted`, delayed balance return, lost rebuy confirmation and season transition. Source comparison found selection, banking, funding and season-clock logic unchanged between the two commits; main adds resync diagnostics and bounds announced rebuy cooldowns. [Funding source][funding], [join loop][join], [installed handler][installed-handler], [main handler][handler], [recovery][recovery].

The checked sources do not establish the matchmaking distribution, time-to-seat improvement from Pro priority, child inheritance of paid benefits, public equal-score tie ordering, or the causal effect of changing selection/banking. Collect passive timing and public rank-series evidence first; specify a controlled comparison before choosing exposure changes. Preserve the five-lineage design and unchanged evidence gate; do not revive a fixed protected-trio/experiment-pair policy that ADR 0002 retires when lineages differ.
